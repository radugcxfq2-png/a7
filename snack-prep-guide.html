The Snack Prep Guide

One hour on a Sunday is enough to fill a fridge shelf with snacks for the working week. Here is the order to do it in, and how long each thing keeps.

The short version

  • Build every snack from a fibre food and a protein food.
  • Prep five things, not fifteen. Repetition is what makes it stick.
  • Store cut vegetables in water and dips in small pots.
  • Most prepped snacks keep four to five days in the fridge.

Why prepping snacks works

Snacks go wrong at the moment of hunger. When you are already hungry, you choose whatever takes the least effort, and in most kitchens that is a packet. Prepping moves the decision to a calm Sunday afternoon, when you can think about what you would actually like to be eating on Wednesday at four.

It also makes portions honest. A pot that holds thirty grams of almonds is a portion. An open bag on the sofa is not.

The two-part formula

Every snack that keeps you going has two halves. One half is fibre and volume: fruit, raw vegetables, oats or wholegrain crackers. The other half is protein: yogurt, cheese, eggs, hummus, beans or nuts. Either half alone is fine for twenty minutes. Together they carry you to the next meal.

  • Apple with cheese or nut butter
  • Carrot and pepper sticks with hummus
  • Berries with plain yogurt and a spoon of oats
  • Wholegrain crackers with a boiled egg
  • A banana with a small handful of almonds

The one-hour plan

  1. Minute 0: boil the eggs. Six eggs, ten minutes from the boil, then straight into cold water. Leave the shells on until you eat them.
  2. Minute 5: wash and cut the vegetables. Carrots, celery and peppers into sticks. Stand carrots and celery upright in a jar of water; keep peppers dry in a lined box.
  3. Minute 20: portion the dry things. Nuts, seeds, popcorn and crackers into small pots or bags, one portion each.
  4. Minute 30: make one dip. Blend a tin of chickpeas with lemon, garlic, tahini and a little water. Divide between four small pots.
  5. Minute 40: roll a batch of oat bites. Oats, nut butter, honey and raisins, mixed and rolled into balls. Half go in the fridge, half in the freezer.
  6. Minute 55: label and stack. Everything on one shelf at eye level, with the day you made it written on the lid.

How long prepped snacks keep

Getting the portion right

Use your own hand as the measure. A cupped handful is a portion of nuts or dried fruit. Two cupped handfuls is a portion of popcorn or raw vegetables. A piece of cheese the size of two thumbs is plenty. Children use their own, smaller hands, which scales the portion without any arithmetic.

If you want numbers for the shopping list, the Snack Portion Planner turns people and days into grams.

When prep goes wrong

The most common failure is ambition. Fifteen different snacks in week one means a tired cook and a bin full of leftovers in week two. Start with five things you already like, repeat them for a month, then swap one at a time. The second failure is hiding the results. Prepped food at the back of the bottom shelf may as well not exist, so give it the best spot in the fridge.
